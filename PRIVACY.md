# KeyBit privacy policy

Last updated: October 8, 2026

KeyBit is a password manager that runs entirely in your browser. This policy explains what it handles and where that data goes.

## What KeyBit stores

KeyBit stores the logins you save: website addresses, names, usernames, passwords, notes, and the last five passwords of each login. They are encrypted with a key derived from your master password (AES-256-GCM) and saved in your browser's extension storage on your device. KeyBit also stores a few settings that are not secret: the sites where you turned KeyBit off and the date of your last backup.

Your master password is never stored. Without it, KeyBit cannot decrypt your logins, and it cannot recover a forgotten master password.

## What KeyBit reads on websites

To suggest and save logins, KeyBit looks for login, sign-up, and change-password fields on the pages you visit. When you submit one of these forms, it reads the username and password you entered so it can ask whether to save them. Nothing is saved unless you confirm. Unconfirmed values are discarded within two minutes.

## What KeyBit shares

Nothing. KeyBit has no servers and makes no network requests. It does not collect analytics, usage data, or crash reports, and it does not sell, transfer, or share any data. Encrypted backup files are created only when you choose **Export**, and they are saved where you choose.

## Removing your data

Removing KeyBit from your browser deletes its stored vault and settings. Export a backup first if you want to keep your logins.
